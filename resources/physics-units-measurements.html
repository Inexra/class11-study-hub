Units and Measurements

Measurements require both magnitude and units. Physics tests laws through unit consistency, uncertainty and well-defined quantities.

Practice Question Paper · 40 marks ↗Full syllabus index

Complete revision notes

1. Introduction

The seven SI base quantities are length (m), mass (kg), time (s), electric current (A), temperature (K), amount of substance (mol) and luminous intensity (cd). Derived units combine base units; a newton is kg m s⁻².

2. The international system of units

Significant figures express precision. Non-zero digits and zeros between significant digits count; leading zeros do not; trailing zeros after a decimal point count. For products/divisions match the fewest significant figures; for sums/subtractions match the least precise decimal place.

3. Significant figures

Dimensions express dependence on base dimensions: velocity [LT⁻¹], acceleration [LT⁻²], force [MLT⁻²], energy [ML²T⁻²], pressure [ML⁻¹T⁻²]. A different unit can represent the same dimension.

4. Dimensions of physical quantities

A homogeneous equation has matching dimensions on each side and in each added term. Dimensional analysis checks equations, infers powers in relations and converts units, but cannot determine numerical constants or prove physical truth.

5. Dimensional formulae and dimensional equations

For Q=AᵃBᵇ, replace quantities by dimensional powers and equate exponents of M, L and T. Ratios of like dimensions are dimensionless. Work and torque share dimensions but are not the same quantity.

Formula sheet

Use SI units, define directions and assumptions before substituting values, show working, and round only the final result.

Worked example

Area of a 2.35 m × 1.2 m rectangle is 2.82 m² before rounding. The width has two significant figures, so report 2.8 m².

Common errors and revision checklist

Do not count leading zeros as significant; retain guard digits until the end; dimensional correctness is necessary but not sufficient.

  • Write the relevant law before substituting values.
  • Show steps, units and signs or directions.
  • Check that the final answer is physically reasonable.
