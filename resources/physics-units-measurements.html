Units & Measurements

A compact revision guide to SI units, dimensional formulae, significant figures and measurement errors.

Back to library

SI base quantities

The seven SI base units are metre (m), kilogram (kg), second (s), ampere (A), kelvin (K), mole (mol) and candela (cd). Derived units combine base units; for example, velocity is m s⁻¹ and force is kg m s⁻² (newton).

Dimensional formulae to remember

  • Velocity: [LT⁻¹]
  • Acceleration: [LT⁻²]
  • Force: [MLT⁻²]
  • Work / energy: [ML²T⁻²]
  • Power: [ML²T⁻³]
  • Pressure / stress: [ML⁻¹T⁻²]

Dimensional analysis can check equation consistency and derive relations up to a dimensionless constant. It cannot determine pure numbers such as 2 or π.

Errors and significant figures

Absolute error is the magnitude of the difference between measured and mean values. Relative error = absolute error / mean value. Percentage error = relative error × 100%.

For multiplication/division: fractional errors approximately add.

For addition/subtraction, absolute errors add in the maximum-error approach. Significant figures communicate measurement precision; leading zeros are not significant, while zeros between non-zero digits are significant.

Practice questions

  1. Write the dimensional formula of pressure.
  2. Convert 72 km h⁻¹ to m s⁻¹.
  3. A length is measured as 2.50 m. How many significant figures are shown?
  4. Two measurements are 4.2 cm and 3.1 cm, each with maximum absolute error 0.1 cm. Find the sum and its maximum absolute error.
  5. Check dimensionally whether s = ut + ½at² is consistent.
Answer check
  1. [ML⁻¹T⁻²]
  2. 20 m s⁻¹
  3. 3 significant figures
  4. 7.3 ± 0.2 cm
  5. Yes: each term has dimension of length.
