CLASS 11 · PHYSICS · CHAPTER 14

Waves

Waves transfer energy through a medium or field without permanently transporting the medium as a whole.

Practice Question Paper · 40 marks ↗Full syllabus index

Complete revision notes

1. Introduction

Mechanical waves travel through a medium; electromagnetic waves do not require one. Medium particles oscillate about equilibrium as the disturbance travels.

2. Transverse and longitudinal waves

Transverse particles oscillate perpendicular to wave travel; longitudinal particles oscillate parallel, creating compressions and rarefactions. Sound in air is longitudinal.

3. Displacement relation in a progressive wave

A progressive sinusoidal wave may be y=A sin(kx−ωt+φ) for travel in +x, with k=2π/λ and ω=2πf.

4. The speed of a travelling wave

Wave speed v=fλ=ω/k. In a given medium, speed is set by the medium and wave type; frequency and wavelength change inversely if speed stays constant.

5. The principle of superposition of waves

Superposition adds displacements algebraically. In-phase waves interfere constructively; opposite-phase waves can partially or fully cancel.

6. Reflection of waves

At a fixed end reflected pulses invert; at a free end they do not. Opposite travelling waves can form standing waves with nodes and antinodes.

7. Beats

Beats occur when two close frequencies superpose; f_beat=|f₁−f₂|, giving periodic loudness variations.

Formula sheet

v=fλ=ω/k; k=2π/λ; ω=2πf; f_beat=|f₁−f₂|; y=A sin(kx−ωt+φ) travels in +x.

Use SI units, state assumptions and directions before substitution, show working and round only the final result.

Worked example

Two tuning forks at 256Hz and 260Hz produce f_beat=4Hz, so loudness maxima recur four times per second.

Common errors and exam checklist

Distinguish wave speed from particle speed; check phase sign for travel direction; beats need close frequencies.

  • Write the relevant law before substituting values.
  • Show steps, units and signs/directions.
  • Check that the final answer is physically reasonable.
