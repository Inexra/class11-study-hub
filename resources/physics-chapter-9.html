CLASS 11 · PHYSICS · CHAPTER 9

Mechanical Properties of Fluids

Fluid mechanics explains pressure and the motion of liquids and gases, including viscous and surface effects.

Practice Question Paper · 40 marks ↗Full syllabus index

Complete revision notes

1. Introduction

A fluid flows and cannot sustain static shear stress. Density is ρ=m/V; relative density compares a substance's density to a reference.

2. Pressure

Pressure is normal force per area. In a stationary fluid at depth h, p=p₀+ρgh. At equal depth, connected fluid pressure is equal irrespective of vessel shape.

3. Streamline flow

Streamline flow is smooth; steady flow means velocity at a point does not vary with time. For steady incompressible flow A₁v₁=A₂v₂. Turbulence is more likely at high Reynolds number.

4. Bernoulli's principle

Bernoulli's relation p+½ρv²+ρgh=constant applies to steady, incompressible, non-viscous flow along a streamline. At comparable height, faster flow has lower static pressure under these assumptions.

5. Viscosity

Viscosity describes internal resistance between fluid layers. Stokes' drag on a small sphere in slow laminar flow is 6πηrv; terminal speed occurs when drag balances effective weight.

6. Surface tension

Surface tension is force per length or energy per area. Excess pressure is 2S/r for a liquid drop and 4S/r for a soap bubble because it has two surfaces.

Formula sheet

p=p₀+ρgh; A₁v₁=A₂v₂; p+½ρv²+ρgh=constant; F_drag=6πηrv; Δp_drop=2S/r; Δp_bubble=4S/r.

Worked example

Water speeds from 2 to 6 m/s in a horizontal pipe. Continuity gives A₂/A₁=v₁/v₂=1/3; Bernoulli predicts lower static pressure in the faster section.

Common errors and exam checklist

Distinguish absolute from gauge pressure; Bernoulli has assumptions; a soap bubble has two surfaces unlike a liquid drop.
