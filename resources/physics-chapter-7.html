CLASS 11 · PHYSICS · CHAPTER 7

Gravitation

Gravitation is the mutual attraction of masses and explains falling bodies, planetary motion and satellite orbits.

Practice Question Paper · 40 marks ↗Full syllabus index

Complete revision notes

1. Introduction

Gravity is an attractive interaction. Near Earth's surface it is approximated by a constant downward acceleration g, but g varies with distance from Earth's centre.

2. Kepler's laws

Kepler's laws: planets orbit in ellipses with the Sun at one focus; the radius vector sweeps equal areas in equal times; and T² is proportional to the cube of semi-major axis for objects orbiting the same central mass.

3. Universal law of gravitation

Newton's law is F=Gm₁m₂/r² along the line joining the centres. Outside a spherically symmetric body, its field is equivalent to that of a point mass at the centre.

4. The gravitational constant

G is universal with SI unit N m² kg⁻²; g is local free-fall acceleration and depends on the attracting body's mass and distance.

5. Acceleration due to gravity of the earth

At a planet surface g=GM/R² and weight W=mg. Mass remains fixed, while weight changes with gravity. Free-fall apparent weight may be zero although gravity is present.

6. Acceleration due to gravity below and above the surface of earth

At altitude h, g_h=g[R/(R+h)]². In a uniform-density Earth model, g_d≈g(1−d/R) below the surface; actual density varies, so this is an approximation.

7. Gravitational potential energy

With zero potential energy at infinity, U=−GMm/r. For small height changes near Earth's surface, ΔU≈mgΔh. Gravitational potential is potential energy per unit mass.

8. Escape speed

Escape speed is v_e=√(2GM/R)=√(2gR), assuming no atmosphere or rotation. It does not depend on the launched body's mass.

9. Earth satellites

For a circular satellite orbit, gravity supplies centripetal force. Equating GMm/r²=mv²/r gives v=√(GM/r) and T=2π√(r³/GM). Measure r from the planet's centre.

10. Energy of an orbiting satellite

For a circular orbit, K=GMm/(2r), U=−GMm/r and total E=−GMm/(2r). Negative total energy indicates a gravitationally bound orbit.

Worked example

At altitude h=R, the distance from Earth's centre is 2R; hence g_h/g=(R/2R)²=1/4. A 600 N weight at the surface becomes approximately 150 N.

Common errors and exam checklist

Use orbital radius from the centre, not altitude alone; distinguish G from g; escape speed is not orbital speed.
