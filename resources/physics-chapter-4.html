Laws of Motion

Newton's laws relate force to motion. Isolate the chosen object, draw all real forces and add them vectorially.

Practice Question Paper · 40 marks ↗Full syllabus index

Complete revision notes

1. Introduction

Aristotle's fallacy attributes sustained motion to a continuing force, but friction hides inertia. Newton's first law says velocity stays constant when net external force is zero; mass measures inertia.

2. Aristotle's fallacy

Newton's second law is ΣF=dp/dt; for constant mass ΣF=ma. Action-reaction forces are equal and opposite but act on different bodies.

3. The law of inertia

Momentum p=mv; impulse J=Δp. Momentum is conserved if net external impulse is zero. Longer stopping time means smaller average force for a fixed momentum change.

4. Newton's first law of motion

Equilibrium needs ΣFₓ=ΣFᵧ=0. Weight points downward; normal reaction is perpendicular to contact; tension acts along a taut string; friction opposes relative motion or its tendency.

5. Newton's second law of motion

Static friction adjusts up to μₛN; kinetic friction is about μₖN. On an incline, weight components are mg sinθ along the plane and mg cosθ normal to it.

6. Newton's third law of motion

Circular motion needs inward resultant ΣFᵣ=mv²/r; centripetal force is supplied by actual forces, not added as a new force.

7. Conservation of momentum

Circular motion needs inward resultant ΣFᵣ=mv²/r; centripetal force is supplied by actual forces, not added as a new force.

8. Equilibrium of a particle

Worked example

Common errors and revision checklist

Action-reaction pairs are on different free-body diagrams; static friction is not always μN; centripetal force is the radial net force.
