Oscillations

Question Paper 1 · 40 marks ↗Full syllabus index

Topic-by-topic explanations

1. Introduction

Periodic motion repeats after a period T; frequency f=1/T and angular frequency ω=2πf. Oscillation is motion to-and-fro about equilibrium, but not every oscillation is SHM.

2. Periodic and oscillatory motions

Simple harmonic motion requires restoring force proportional to displacement and opposite in direction: F=−kx and a=−ω²x. Displacement can be written x=A cos(ωt+φ).

3. Simple harmonic motion

A projection of uniform circular motion onto a diameter follows SHM, with the same angular frequency. This gives a geometric way to understand sinusoidal displacement.

4. Simple harmonic motion and uniform circular motion

For x=A cosωt, velocity is v=−Aω sinωt and acceleration a=−ω²x. Speed is maximum at equilibrium and zero at extremes; acceleration magnitude is maximum at extremes.

5. Velocity and acceleration in simple harmonic motion

For an ideal spring-mass oscillator, ω=√(k/m) and T=2π√(m/k), assuming small linear deformation and negligible damping.

6. Force law for simple harmonic motion

Energy transfers between kinetic energy and spring potential U=½kx². Total energy is ½kA²; K is maximum at equilibrium and U is maximum at the extreme positions.

7. Energy in simple harmonic motion

For a simple pendulum at small angles, T=2π√(l/g), independent of bob mass. This approximation assumes small amplitude and negligible damping.

8. The Simple Pendulum

Real oscillators are damped by friction or resistance, so amplitude and mechanical energy decrease. A driving force can maintain oscillations, with resonance when driving frequency approaches the natural frequency.

Formula sheet

Worked example

For m=0.5 kg and k=200 N/m, T=2π√(0.5/200)=0.314 s. With A=0.10 m, maximum speed is Aω=2.0 m/s.

Common errors and revision checklist

Use the small-angle pendulum relation only for small amplitudes. Velocity and acceleration peak at different positions. Damping prevents exact energy conservation.
