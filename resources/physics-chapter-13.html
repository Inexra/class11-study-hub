CLASS 11 · PHYSICS · CHAPTER 13

Oscillations

Oscillations are repeated motions about equilibrium. Simple harmonic motion has a linear restoring force directed toward equilibrium.

Practice Question Paper · 40 marks ↗Full syllabus index

Complete revision notes

1. Introduction

Periodic motion repeats after period T, with frequency f=1/T and angular frequency ω=2πf. Oscillation need not always be SHM.

2. Periodic and oscillatory motions

In SHM, F=−kx and a=−ω²x. A solution is x=Acos(ωt+φ), where A is amplitude and φ phase.

3. Simple harmonic motion

A projection of uniform circular motion onto a diameter performs SHM with the same angular frequency.

4. Simple harmonic motion and uniform circular motion

For x=Acosωt, v=−Aωsinωt and a=−ω²x. Speed is maximum at equilibrium; it is zero at extremes. Acceleration magnitude is maximum at extremes.

5. Velocity and acceleration in simple harmonic motion

A spring oscillator has ω=√(k/m), T=2π√(m/k) if damping is negligible and Hooke's law holds.

6. Force law for simple harmonic motion

Energy alternates between kinetic and spring potential U=½kx². Total E=½kA² remains constant without damping.

7. Energy in simple harmonic motion

For a simple pendulum at small angle, T=2π√(l/g), approximately independent of bob mass. This requires a small amplitude and negligible damping.

8. The Simple Pendulum

For a simple pendulum at small angle, T=2π√(l/g), approximately independent of bob mass. This requires a small amplitude and negligible damping.

Formula sheet

x=Acos(ωt+φ); a=−ω²x; T=2π/ω; spring T=2π√(m/k); pendulum T=2π√(l/g); E=½kA².

Use SI units, state assumptions and directions before substitution, show working and round only the final result.

Worked example

A spring with m=.5 kg,k=200 N/m has T=2π√(.5/200)≈0.314 s.

Common errors and exam checklist

SHM needs a linear restoring force; pendulum formula assumes small angle; velocity and acceleration vanish at different positions.

  • Write the relevant law before substituting values.
  • Show steps, units and signs/directions.
  • Check that the final answer is physically reasonable.
