Oscillations

Topic-by-topic revision notes matched to the syllabus, with a formula sheet and worked example.

Question Paper 1 · 40 marks ↗Full syllabus index

Topic-by-topic explanations

1. Introduction

Periodic motion repeats after a fixed time T; frequency is f=1/T and angular frequency ω=2πf. Oscillation is to-and-fro motion about equilibrium, but not all oscillations are SHM.

2. Periodic and oscillatory motions

An oscillating body moves about its equilibrium position. Its period is the time for a full cycle and its amplitude is the maximum displacement from equilibrium.

3. Simple harmonic motion

Simple harmonic motion has restoring force proportional to displacement and opposite in direction: F=−kx and a=−ω²x. Displacement can be written x=Acos(ωt+φ).

4. Simple harmonic motion and uniform circular motion

A projection of uniform circular motion onto a diameter follows SHM with the same angular frequency; the projection gives sinusoidal displacement.

5. Velocity and acceleration in simple harmonic motion

For x=Acosωt, v=−Aωsinωt and a=−ω²x. Speed is maximum at equilibrium and zero at extremes; acceleration magnitude is maximum at extremes.

6. Force law for simple harmonic motion

A linear spring obeys Hooke's law F=−kx. With mass m, ω=√(k/m), giving T=2π√(m/k) if damping is negligible.

7. Energy in simple harmonic motion

Energy alternates between kinetic energy and spring potential energy U=½kx². Total energy E=½kA²; kinetic energy is maximum at equilibrium and potential energy at extremes.

8. The Simple Pendulum

For a simple pendulum at small angles, T=2π√(l/g), independent of bob mass. The approximation assumes small amplitude and negligible damping.

Formula sheet

x=Acos(ωt+φ); a=−ω²x; T=2π/ω; T_spring=2π√(m/k); T_pendulum=2π√(l/g); E=½kA².

Worked example

For m=0.5 kg and k=200 N/m, T=2π√(0.5/200)≈0.314 s. At amplitude 0.10 m, maximum speed Aω=2.0 m/s.

Common errors and revision checklist

A linear restoring force is required for SHM. Use the pendulum period only for small angles. Velocity and acceleration peak at different positions.

  • Write the governing law before substituting values.
  • Show units and signs/directions.
  • Check whether assumptions are satisfied.
