CLASS 11 · PHYSICS · CHAPTER 6

System of Particles and Rotational Motion

Centre-of-mass dynamics describes translation; rotational dynamics uses angular variables and moment of inertia about a specified axis.

Practice Question Paper · 40 marks ↗Full syllabus index

Complete revision notes

1. Introduction

Centre of mass is r_cm=Σmᵢrᵢ/Σmᵢ. Total momentum P=Mv_cm and M a_cm=F_ext. An isolated system conserves its momentum.

2. Centre of mass

The vector product A×B has magnitude ABsinθ and right-hand-rule direction. Torque τ=r×F; angular momentum L=r×p. For constant I about a fixed axis, τ=Iα and L=Iω.

3. Motion of centre of mass

Angular speed ω=dθ/dt; angular acceleration α=dω/dt. Rolling without slipping gives v=rω and tangential acceleration rα; centripetal acceleration is rω².

4. Linear momentum of a system of particles

Rigid-body equilibrium requires ΣF=0 and Στ=0. Torque magnitude is rFsinθ, where θ is the angle between radius vector and force.

5. Vector product of two vectors

Moment of inertia I=Σmᵢrᵢ² depends on mass distribution and axis. Parallel-axis theorem I=I_cm+Md²; for a plane lamina I_z=I_x+I_y.

6. Angular velocity and its relation with linear velocity

Rotational kinetic energy is ½Iω². With no external torque, angular momentum is conserved; if I decreases, ω increases for fixed L.

7. Torque and angular momentum

Rotational kinetic energy is ½Iω². With no external torque, angular momentum is conserved; if I decreases, ω increases for fixed L.

8. Equilibrium of a rigid body

Rotational kinetic energy is ½Iω². With no external torque, angular momentum is conserved; if I decreases, ω increases for fixed L.

9. Moment of inertia

Rotational kinetic energy is ½Iω². With no external torque, angular momentum is conserved; if I decreases, ω increases for fixed L.

10. Kinematics of rotational motion about a fixed axis

Rotational kinetic energy is ½Iω². With no external torque, angular momentum is conserved; if I decreases, ω increases for fixed L.

11. Dynamics of rotational motion about a fixed axis

Rotational kinetic energy is ½Iω². With no external torque, angular momentum is conserved; if I decreases, ω increases for fixed L.

12. Angular momentum in case of rotations about a fixed axis

Rotational kinetic energy is ½Iω². With no external torque, angular momentum is conserved; if I decreases, ω increases for fixed L.

Formula sheet

x_cm=Σmᵢxᵢ/Σmᵢ; τ=Iα; L=Iω; K_rot=½Iω²; v=rω; I=I_cm+Md².

Worked example

Masses 2 kg at x=0 and 3 kg at x=4 m have x_cm=2.4 m. A 6 N m torque with I=2 kg m² gives α=3 rad/s².

Common errors and revision checklist

Moment of inertia depends on axis; torque uses the perpendicular force component; equilibrium needs both force and torque balance.
