CLASS 11 · PHYSICS · CHAPTER 11

Thermodynamics

Thermodynamics relates heat, work and internal energy, with laws that constrain possible processes and heat engines.

Practice Question Paper · 40 marks ↗Full syllabus index

Complete revision notes

1. Introduction

Thermodynamics describes macroscopic states with variables such as pressure, volume and temperature.

2. Thermal equilibrium

The zeroth law says thermal equilibrium is transitive; it provides the basis for defining and measuring temperature.

3. Zeroth law of thermodynamics

Heat is energy transferred due to temperature difference; work is energy transfer through other macroscopic means. Internal energy is microscopic energy stored in a system.

4. Heat, internal energy and work

Using convention ΔQ=ΔU+W, Q is heat added and W is work done by the system. Other conventions exist; state which is used.

5. First law of thermodynamics

The first law is energy conservation. For a cycle ΔU=0, so net heat added equals net work done by the system under this convention.

6. Specific heat capacity

For a gas, C_v and C_p refer to molar heat capacities at constant volume and pressure. For an ideal gas C_p−C_v=R per mole.

7. Thermodynamic state variables and equation of state

State variables describe equilibrium states; heat and work depend on the path. Ideal-gas equation of state is pV=nRT.

8. Thermodynamic processes

Isothermal means constant T; ideal gas ΔU=0. Adiabatic means Q=0. Isochoric means constant volume so boundary work is zero; isobaric means constant pressure.

9. Second law of thermodynamics

The second law rules out complete conversion of heat absorbed from one reservoir into work in a cycle. Heat naturally flows hot to cold; entropy of an isolated system does not decrease.

10. Reversible and irreversible processes

Reversible processes are idealized quasi-static changes without dissipative effects; friction and heat flow across finite temperature differences are irreversible.

11. Carnot engine

Carnot efficiency η=1−T_c/T_h is the maximum for an engine between two reservoirs; temperatures must be in kelvin.

Formula sheet

ΔQ=ΔU+W (work by system); pV=nRT; ΔU=nC_vΔT; C_p−C_v=R; η_Carnot=1−T_c/T_h.

Use SI units, state assumptions and directions before substitution, show working and round only the final result.

Worked example

Two moles heated at constant volume by 10 K with C_v=20 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹: W=0 and ΔU=Q=nC_vΔT=400 J.

Common errors and exam checklist

State the first-law sign convention; use kelvin in Carnot efficiency; isothermal does not mean no heat transfer, and adiabatic does not mean constant temperature.

  • Write the relevant law before substituting values.
  • Show steps, units and signs/directions.
  • Check that the final answer is physically reasonable.
