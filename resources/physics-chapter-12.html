CLASS 11 · PHYSICS · CHAPTER 12

Kinetic Theory

Kinetic theory connects macroscopic gas properties to molecular motion and statistical averages.

Practice Question Paper · 40 marks ↗Full syllabus index

Complete revision notes

1. Introduction

Matter consists of discrete molecules. Ideal gas assumes molecular volume negligible, intermolecular forces negligible except during collisions, and elastic collisions.

2. Molecular nature of matter

The ideal gas obeys pV=nRT. Pressure is due to molecular momentum transfer to the container walls.

3. Behaviour of gases

Mean translational kinetic energy per molecule is 3k_BT/2, so absolute temperature measures average translational motion.

4. Kinetic theory of an ideal gas

Kinetic theory gives pV=(1/3)Nm⟨c²⟩ and c_rms=√(3k_BT/m)=√(3RT/M).

5. Law of equipartition of energy

Equipartition assigns ½k_BT per active quadratic degree of freedom per molecule. A monatomic ideal gas has three translational degrees of freedom.

6. Specific heat capacity

For f active degrees, ideal-gas internal energy U=(f/2)nRT and C_v=fR/2. Monatomic gas has C_v=3R/2 and C_p=5R/2.

7. Mean free path

Mean free path is average distance between molecular collisions. It increases as number density/pressure decreases if other conditions stay comparable.

Formula sheet

pV=nRT; ⟨K_trans⟩=3k_BT/2; pV=(1/3)Nm⟨c²⟩; c_rms=√(3RT/M); U=(f/2)nRT.

Use SI units, state assumptions and directions before substitution, show working and round only the final result.

Worked example

For nitrogen M=.028 kg/mol at 300K, c_rms=√(3RT/M)≈517 m/s.

Common errors and exam checklist

Use Kelvin; distinguish rms speed from mean speed; equipartition counts active degrees of freedom within the applicable temperature range.
