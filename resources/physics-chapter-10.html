CLASS 11 · PHYSICS · CHAPTER 10

Thermal Properties of Matter

Thermal physics studies temperature, heat, expansion, calorimetry, phase changes, heat transfer and cooling.

Practice Question Paper · 40 marks ↗Full syllabus index

Complete revision notes

1. Introduction

Temperature determines the direction of net heat transfer. Heat is energy transferred because of a temperature difference, not a substance stored inside a body.

2. Temperature and heat

Thermometers use a thermometric property such as liquid volume or electrical resistance. T(K)=t(°C)+273.15.

3. Measurement of temperature

For a fixed amount of ideal gas, pV=nRT. Absolute temperature is proportional to average translational kinetic energy.

4. Ideal-gas equation and absolute temperature

Linear expansion ΔL=αLΔT and volume expansion ΔV=γVΔT. For isotropic solids and small changes, γ≈3α. Restrained expansion may generate stress.

5. Thermal expansion

Specific heat capacity c gives Q=mcΔT when no phase change. Heat capacity C=mc refers to a whole object.

6. Specific heat capacity

Calorimetry applies energy conservation: heat lost equals heat gained by other bodies if the system is insulated. Include containers when their heat capacity matters.

7. Calorimetry

During a phase change, Q=mL is absorbed or released while temperature stays constant at the transition pressure.

8. Change of state

Conduction transfers energy via interactions, convection by bulk fluid motion and radiation by electromagnetic waves. Vacuum stops conduction and convection but not radiation.

9. Heat transfer

Newton's law of cooling approximates cooling rate as proportional to the excess temperature over surroundings when differences are small and conditions remain similar.

10. Newton's law of cooling

Separate sensible heat mcΔT from latent heat mL, check units and account for heat loss in practical situations.

Formula sheet

T(K)=t(°C)+273.15; ΔL=αLΔT; Q=mcΔT; Q=mL; pV=nRT; cooling rate ∝ T−T_s.

Use SI units, state assumptions and directions before substitution, show working and round only the final result.

Worked example

Heating 0.5 kg water by 10 K, c=4200 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹: Q=mcΔT=21,000 J.

Common errors and exam checklist

Use Kelvin for gas-law ratios; use latent heat during phase change; include all objects in a calorimetry energy balance.

  • Write the relevant law before substituting values.
  • Show steps, units and signs/directions.
  • Check that the final answer is physically reasonable.
