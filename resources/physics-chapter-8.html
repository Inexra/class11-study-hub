CLASS 11 · PHYSICS · CHAPTER 8

Mechanical Properties of Solids

Solid mechanics quantifies how materials deform under applied force and how their elastic response supports safe design.

Practice Question Paper · 40 marks ↗Full syllabus index

Complete revision notes

1. Introduction

Elastic deformation is recovered after unloading within the elastic limit; plastic deformation leaves permanent change.

2. Stress and strain

Stress is restoring force per unit area. Normal stress acts perpendicular to area; shear stress acts tangentially. Its SI unit is pascal.

3. Hooke's law

Longitudinal strain is ΔL/L, volume strain ΔV/V and shear strain is approximately angular distortion in radians. Strain is dimensionless.

4. Stress-strain curve

Within the proportional limit, Hooke's law says stress is proportional to strain; its proportionality constant depends on deformation type and material.

5. Elastic moduli

A typical ductile stress-strain curve may show proportional limit, elastic limit, yield region, ultimate stress and fracture. Brittle materials fracture with little plastic deformation.

6. Applications of elastic behaviour of materials

Young's modulus Y=longitudinal stress/longitudinal strain; bulk modulus B=−Δp/(ΔV/V); shear modulus is shear stress/shear strain. In tension, ΔL=FL/(AY).

Formula sheet

Stress=F/A; strain=ΔL/L; Y=FL/(AΔL); B=−Δp/(ΔV/V); elastic energy density=½(stress)(strain).

Use SI units, state assumptions and directions before substitution, show working and round only the final result.

Worked example

Wire: L=2 m, A=10⁻⁶ m², Y=2×10¹¹ Pa, F=100 N. Extension ΔL=FL/(AY)=0.001 m.

Common errors and exam checklist

Stress has units of pascals; strain is dimensionless. Use original length and area, and apply Hooke's law only within its range.

  • Show steps, units and signs/directions.
  • Check that the final answer is physically reasonable.
