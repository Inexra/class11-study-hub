CLASS 11 · PHYSICS · CHAPTER 3

Motion in a Plane

Resolve two-dimensional motion into perpendicular vector components and solve the components using the same time interval.

Practice Question Paper · 40 marks ↗Full syllabus index

Complete revision notes

1. Introduction

Scalars have magnitude only; vectors have magnitude and direction. Multiplication by a negative scalar reverses a vector; subtraction adds the negative vector.

2. Scalars and vectors

Use head-to-tail or parallelogram methods for graphical addition. At angle θ from +x: Aₓ=Acosθ and Aᵧ=Asinθ.

3. Multiplication of vectors by real numbers

Add components independently: Rₓ=ΣAₓ and Rᵧ=ΣAᵧ; R=√(Rₓ²+Rᵧ²). The component signs determine the correct quadrant.

4. Addition and subtraction of vectors – graphical method

Without air resistance, projectile horizontal acceleration is zero and vertical acceleration is −g. For equal launch and landing heights: T=2u sinθ/g, H=u²sin²θ/(2g), R=u²sin2θ/g.

5. Resolution of vectors

In uniform circular motion speed is constant but velocity changes direction. Centripetal acceleration is inward: a_c=v²/r=ω²r; v=rω.

6. Vector addition – analytical method

In uniform circular motion speed is constant but velocity changes direction. Centripetal acceleration is inward: a_c=v²/r=ω²r; v=rω.

7. Motion in a plane

In uniform circular motion speed is constant but velocity changes direction. Centripetal acceleration is inward: a_c=v²/r=ω²r; v=rω.

8. Motion in a plane with constant acceleration

In uniform circular motion speed is constant but velocity changes direction. Centripetal acceleration is inward: a_c=v²/r=ω²r; v=rω.

9. Projectile motion

In uniform circular motion speed is constant but velocity changes direction. Centripetal acceleration is inward: a_c=v²/r=ω²r; v=rω.

10. Uniform circular motion

In uniform circular motion speed is constant but velocity changes direction. Centripetal acceleration is inward: a_c=v²/r=ω²r; v=rω.

Formula sheet

Aₓ=Acosθ; Aᵧ=Asinθ; T=2u sinθ/g; H=u²sin²θ/(2g); R=u²sin2θ/g; a_c=v²/r.

Use SI units, define directions and assumptions before substituting values, show working, and round only the final result.

Worked example

A 20 m/s projectile at 30°, g=10: uₓ=17.32 m/s, uᵧ=10 m/s; T=2 s, H=5 m, R≈34.6 m.

Common errors and revision checklist

Do not add vector magnitudes blindly; measure angles from the named axis; centripetal acceleration points toward the centre.

  • Write the relevant law before substituting values.
  • Show steps, units and signs or directions.
  • Check that the final answer is physically reasonable.
