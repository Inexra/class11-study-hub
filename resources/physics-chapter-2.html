CLASS 11 · PHYSICS · CHAPTER 2

Motion in a Straight Line

Kinematics describes motion after an origin and positive direction are chosen. Distance/speed are non-negative; displacement/velocity retain direction.

Practice Question Paper · 40 marks ↗Full syllabus index

Complete revision notes

1. Introduction

Position gives location relative to an origin. Displacement is x₂−x₁; distance is total path length and is never less than the magnitude of displacement.

2. Instantaneous velocity and speed

Average velocity is displacement/time; instantaneous velocity v=dx/dt. Average speed uses distance/time and instantaneous speed is |v|. The slope of an x-t graph is velocity.

3. Acceleration

Acceleration a=dv/dt can have either sign. An object slows when acceleration opposes velocity; negative acceleration does not automatically mean slowing down.

4. Kinematic equations for uniformly accelerated motion

For constant acceleration: v=u+at, s=ut+½at², v²=u²+2as and s=(u+v)t/2. These equations assume acceleration is constant.

Formula sheet

v=u+at; s=ut+½at²; v²=u²+2as; a=Δv/Δt.

Use SI units, define directions and assumptions before substituting values, show working, and round only the final result.

Worked example

If u=5 m/s, a=2 m/s² and t=4 s, then v=13 m/s and s=36 m.

Common errors and revision checklist

Use displacement in signed equations; convert km/h to m/s; use these equations only for constant acceleration.

  • Show steps, units and signs or directions.
  • Check that the final answer is physically reasonable.
